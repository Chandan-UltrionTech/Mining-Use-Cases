Exploration &
blast

See the geology. Measure the blast.

From core imagery to fragmentation, vision helps understand the orebody and whether blasting produced the rock size the mine wanted.

01

Automated drill-core analysis

Use core photographs as a digital geological dataset instead of relying only on manual logging.

Emerging / deployed
Camera sees

Core trays, fractures, veins, discing, textures and colour changes.

CV does

Detects and segments geological structures, then measures their location, spacing or class.

Why useful

Faster re-logging, more consistent geotechnical data, better targeting and hazard mapping.

Real example: Datarock applied computer vision to about 85,000 m of drill-core imagery at AngloGold Ashanti's Sunrise Dam to identify core discing associated with high stress. [S1]
02

Ore / waste and grade mapping

Classify exposed rock before or during excavation.

Pilot / field tested
Camera sees

Mine faces using RGB, hyperspectral or multispectral imagery.

CV does

Maps mineral signatures and classifies pixels/regions into ore, waste or grade classes.

Why useful

Better grade control, reduced dilution and more informed excavation decisions.

Real example: hyperspectral OreSense technology was field-tested at CITIC Pacific's Sino Iron and AngloGold Ashanti's Tropicana operations in Western Australia. [S2]
03

Blast fragmentation analysis

Automatically measure rock-size distribution after blasting.

Commercially mature
Camera sees

Muck piles or blasted material while it is being loaded.

CV does

Finds fragment boundaries and estimates particle-size distribution such as P80.

Why useful

Shows whether blast design is producing material that is easy to load, crush and process.

Real example: Newmont's Akyem Gold Mine installed Orica FRAGTrack cameras on shovels; Orica reported a 17.8% fragmentation improvement. [S3]

Loading &
mine safety

Protect the machine. Protect the operator.

Vision can watch the shovel and the people around mobile equipment continuously, turning visible hazards into alerts before they become incidents.

04

Shovel bucket, tooth and boulder monitoring

Watch the loading tool continuously instead of discovering failures downstream.

Commercially mature
Camera sees

Bucket teeth, bucket contents, large rocks and the digging cycle.

CV does

Detects missing/broken teeth, identifies boulders and can estimate fragmentation.

Why useful

Prevents broken teeth from reaching crushers and reduces unplanned downtime.

Real example: Weir Motion Metrics reported ShovelMetrics use at Sishen Iron Ore Mine for missing-tooth detection and fragmentation monitoring. [S4]
05

Driver fatigue and distraction detection

Monitor the operator, not just the vehicle.

Commercially mature
Camera sees

Eyes, eyelids, gaze, head pose and face behaviour inside the cab.

CV does

Detects drowsiness, microsleeps and distraction events.

Why useful

Triggers immediate warnings and gives safety teams evidence for fatigue interventions.

Real example: MMG deployed Hexagon's Operator Alertness System on underground trucks at Rosebery Mine in Tasmania. [S5]
06

PPE, exclusion-zone and worker-equipment proximity monitoring

Turn existing CCTV into a real-time safety layer.

Commercially available
Camera sees

Workers, helmets/vests, vehicles and defined hazardous zones.

CV does

Detects missing PPE, tracks people/vehicles and identifies dangerous zone entry or proximity.

Why useful

Allows supervisors to act before a collision or unsafe exposure becomes an incident.

Real example: Solntsevsky coal mine used AI CCTV for PPE compliance; other mining deployments use dynamic video-based exclusion zones. [S6]

Material
handling

Keep the material stream healthy.

On conveyors and at transfer points, computer vision measures rock size and catches unwanted material before it disrupts downstream equipment.

07

Conveyor foreign-object and oversize detection

Inspect every tonne of moving material without a person standing beside the belt.

Commercially mature
Camera sees

Ore on a conveyor, including oversized rocks, metal pieces and other unwanted objects.

CV does

Detects anomalies or known object classes in real time.

Why useful

Alerts operators or stops the belt before crushers, chutes or downstream equipment are damaged.

Real example: BHP has reported AI-based Oversize and Foreign Object Detection in Western Australia Iron Ore operations. [S7]
08

Online particle-size monitoring

Measure material size continuously on belts instead of relying only on manual samples.

Commercially mature
Camera sees

Rock travelling on conveyors or through transfer points.

CV does

Segments particles and estimates coarse particle-size distribution.

Why useful

Supports crusher, screen and mill optimisation and can reveal changes in upstream blasting.

Real example: Metso RockSense 2D is a camera-based online particle-size analyser for crushing and conveying applications. [S8]
09

Optical / laser ore sorting

Classify particles and physically separate valuable material from waste or contaminants.

Commercially mature
Camera sees

Individual particles under optical, laser, NIR or related imaging sensors.

CV does

Classifies each particle according to visible/spectral characteristics.

Why useful

Rejects waste before expensive downstream processing and protects product quality.

Real example: Mt Cattlin lithium mine installed TOMRA laser sorting to reject basalt contamination before dense-media separation. [S9]

Processing &
inspection

Watch the process. Inspect the places people should not enter.

In processing and inspection, vision moves beyond object detection into process measurement, 3D reconstruction and large-area anomaly detection.

10

Flotation froth monitoring

Use vision to measure what operators traditionally judge by eye.

Commercially mature
Camera sees

Froth surface, bubbles, colour, texture and motion in flotation cells.

CV does

Measures bubble size, froth velocity, stability, colour/texture and related process features.

Why useful

Provides continuous process feedback for flotation control and optimisation.

Real example: Metso FrothSense+ uses camera-based measurements of flotation froth characteristics for process monitoring. [S10]
11

Drone 3D survey, stockpile and underground stope inspection

Convert many overlapping images into measurable 3D mine geometry.

Commercially mature
Camera sees

Stockpiles, pits, benches, stopes and inaccessible underground openings.

CV does

Photogrammetry reconstructs 3D surfaces; repeated surveys reveal volume and shape changes.

Why useful

Volume measurement, overbreak detection and safer inspection without putting people into hazardous areas.

Real example: Barrick's Golden Sunlight Mine used underground drone imagery and Pix4D photogrammetry to inspect stopes and identify loose material/overbreak. [S11]
12

Thermal / visual field inspection

Find process anomalies across large mine areas that are slow or unsafe to inspect manually.

Deployed / expanding
Camera sees

RGB and thermal imagery of leach fields, pipelines, ponds, slopes or remote infrastructure.

CV does

Detects dry zones, leaks, temperature anomalies or visual changes and geolocates them.

Why useful

Turns large-area inspection into targeted maintenance instead of long manual patrols.

Real example: SQM used autonomous thermal/visual drone inspection over large Atacama operations to identify irrigation/leaching issues and direct field teams. [S12]

Research rule

Only applications with documented real-world products, mine-site projects, field trials or deployments were used to define the main use cases. Systems that are purely GPS, radar, LiDAR or generic “AI” were not counted as computer vision unless cameras/images were an explicit part of the perception workflow.

The practical opportunity is not “computer vision for mining” as one project. It is choosing one mining decision that can be made better from images.
